# Global-Superstore-Analysis-Report-2011-2014-

This project analyzes the performance of a Global Superstore between 2011 and 2014 using Microsoft Power BI.

The analysis focuses on sales, profit, product categories, customer segments, regional and country performance, shipping, discounts, customer concentration, and market-specific profitability.

The project was structured as a complete business-intelligence story, moving from overall performance to regional, country, city, customer, and strategic growth analysis.

Business Objective

The main analytical question was:

What factors drove profitability, and what actions should management take to improve future performance?

The project was designed to answer questions such as:

Has revenue growth translated into sustainable profitability?

Which customer segments contribute most to profit?

Which product categories generate the strongest returns?

Which regions and countries drive business success?

Which markets underperform despite strong sales?

How do shipping costs and discounts influence profitability?

Which cities and countries should receive more investment?

Which customers contribute disproportionately to revenue?

What strategic actions could improve growth and profitability?

Key Findings

1. Overall Performance Improved Over Time

The project observations indicate that sales and profit trended upward from 2011 to 2014, with 2014 recording the highest sales performance.

The strongest profit contribution came from the Consumer segment, followed by Corporate, while Home Office contributed the least.

2. Technology Was the Strongest Category

Technology profit — approximately $663.89K

Office Supplies profit — approximately $518.48K

Furniture profit — approximately $285.18K

At subcategory level, Tables recorded a negative profit of approximately -$64.08K.

3. Profitability Varied Significantly Across Regions

Strong displayed regional profits included:

Central — approximately $311.38K

North — approximately $194.72K

North Asia — approximately $165.58K

South — approximately $140.36K

Central Asia — approximately $132.48K

4. The United States Was a Major Profit Market

The United States dashboard reports approximately $0.29M total profit.

Profit trend:

2011 — $50K

2012 — $62K

2013 — $82K

2014 — $93K

Detroit emerged as the most profitable city by average profit in the displayed city comparison, while Concord was the least profitable.

6. Nigeria Experienced Declining Profitability

Nigeria's 2014 profit trend:

Q1 — approximately -$14.5K

Q2 — approximately -$16.4K

Q3 — approximately -$23.2K

Q4 — approximately -$26.6K

The dashboard also shows a 70% average discount for 2014.

7. Southeast Asia Showed Uneven Country Performance

Stronger displayed countries included:

Malaysia — approximately $16K

Indonesia — approximately $16K

Singapore — approximately $9K

Cambodia — approximately $4K

Negative results were shown for Vietnam, Myanmar, Thailand, and the Philippines.

8. Australia Was a Strong Market

Australia recorded approximately $103.91K in total profit.

9. Customer and Segment Concentration Matters

A relatively small number of customers contribute a meaningful share of sales, creating opportunities for retention, personalized offers, and cross-selling.

Strategic Recommendations

Increase focus on high-margin Technology products.

Improve Furniture margins through pricing, sourcing, and inventory review.

Concentrate investment in top-performing regions and countries.

Reduce logistics and shipping inefficiencies.

Strengthen loyalty and retention initiatives for top customers.

Localize product and promotional strategies by market.

Reduce dependence on a limited number of markets and customers.

Monitor persistently underperforming countries and cities.

Conduct root-cause analysis on Nigeria's declining profitability.

Use high-performing markets such as Detroit and Australia as benchmarks.

Conclusion

The Global Superstore experienced strong overall growth between 2011 and 2014, supported by Technology products, Consumer customers, and a number of high-performing markets.

However, profitability was uneven across countries, regions, categories, and cities. The main strategic message is that continued growth should be supported by market-specific, category-specific, and customer-specific decisions rather than a single global strategy.
